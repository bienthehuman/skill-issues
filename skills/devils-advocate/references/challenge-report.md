# Challenge Report

If consuming upstream artifacts, place this before the title:
`Upstream: <names> | Re-derived: <load-bearing claims and actual source checks>`.
Name failed/unavailable re-derivations and dependent limitations. Omit the envelope
when none were consumed. Keep plain Markdown proportional to the decision;
short fields suffice for simple ideas. Do not fill sections with generic objections.

## Idea or decision being challenged

Original claim, proposed direction, or plan and its scope.

## Intended goal and success conditions

Problem, intended outcome, beneficiaries/affected people, relevant constraints,
and what successful adoption or implementation would accomplish, with sources.
If no meaningful goal can be established, ask what success looks like before
giving a verdict; retain supported context and questions without inventing a goal.

## Context and evidence

Available primary material and user-attributed context, including meaningful
supporting evidence and contrary evidence. Untagged findings name directly
inspected support; attribute user reports without implying independent verification.
`inferred` means concluded from observed facts; `assumed` means an unverified
premise; `unknown` means insufficient evidence with no value offered.
Independently re-derive load-bearing upstream claims. Shared-origin artifacts
count as one source; the proposing agent's rationale is not independent evidence.
Name actual checks and missing sources. Conclusions not re-derived remain
`inferred` and name the inherited claim. Never imply unavailable repository
inspection or verification happened.

## Load-bearing assumptions

Include hidden assumptions whose failure would materially alter the recommendation.

| Assumption | Evidence basis and classification | Dependent conclusion | Evidence that would establish or challenge it |
|---|---|---|---|

Re-check every load-bearing inferred/assumed claim against available sources;
surface every assumption relied on in open questions. No speculative padding.

## Strongest counter-case

Strongest credible argument against this direction under the actual goal and
evidence, with relevant opportunity costs, complexity, maintenance, operational
constraints, or unintended consequences. Distinguish plausible likelihood and
consequence from theoretical possibility. Weigh evidence supporting the proposal
as seriously as evidence against it. State what the counter-case establishes and
its limits; the strongest counterargument is not automatically the winning argument.

For consequential product or feature directions, compare supported proposal value
against worthwhile work displaced by pursuing it, including meaningful costs of
delaying either the proposal or its alternatives. Do not require formal business
analysis, numerical ROI, or invented estimates.

## Incentives and adoption friction

Relevant workflows, training/migration costs, stakeholder incentives, ownership,
support and maintenance responsibilities, and organizational/operational capacity.
Use available evidence; do not invent user behavior or stakeholder preferences.
Identify decisive unverified adoption premises and the evidence needed. State
when a lens is irrelevant or unavailable rather than fabricating concerns.

## Meaningful alternatives or status quo

Simpler interventions, current alternatives, or doing nothing where meaningful,
assessed against the same goal. No forced option count or implementation bake-off.

## Verdict, rationale, and uncertainty

Choose **Proceed** (sufficiently justified under established constraints),
**Reshape** (worthwhile goal, direction requires material adjustment), or **Drop**
(available evidence does not justify the proposed form). Explain decisive
assumptions, supporting evidence, what the opposing case establishes, and why
the balance supports the verdict. Keep unsupported conclusions explicitly
provisional with dependent gaps; no verdict before the goal is established.
This is a recommendation, not human approval or execution authorization.

## Evidence that would overturn the verdict

Concrete evidence or changed premises that would change this recommendation,
why they would change it, and how they could be checked. Required for every
verdict, including Proceed; avoid vague promises to reconsider if circumstances change.

## Recommended next action or investigation

Smallest justified action or investigation and its decision-changing result.
Distinguish a recommended action from permission to execute it.

## Open questions and limitations

Every assumption relied on, consequential unresolved question, missing source,
failed check/re-derivation, and the conclusion it limits; name evidence or human
clarification needed. Missing evidence is not proof for or against the proposal.
If the template or inputs are missing, disclose partial coverage.
Optional Requirements/Decision Record consumers receive verdict, assumptions,
and overturning evidence and independently check whether assumptions still hold.
No sibling dependency, automatic pipeline, implementation plan, impact analysis,
code review, or behavioral reliability claim.
